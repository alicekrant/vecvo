01 — Aging Biology

Hallmarks & Interventions

Cellular and molecular basis of biological aging: genomic instability, epigenetic drift, telomere attrition, proteostasis failure, deregulated nutrient sensing, mitochondrial dysfunction, cellular senescence, stem cell exhaustion, and altered intercellular communication. The most evidence-dense domain in longevity science.

02 — Oncogenesis

Cancer as Aging Disease

Cancer incidence increases exponentially with age. Age-associated DNA damage accumulation, immune senescence reducing immunosurveillance, pro-inflammatory SASP creating permissive tumor microenvironments, and epigenetic reprogramming all converge to drive oncogenesis. Prevention and precision interventions reviewed.

03 — Neurodegeneration

Alzheimer, Parkinson & Beyond

Neurodegenerative diseases are unified by age-dependent proteostasis collapse, mitochondrial failure, neuroinflammation mediated by activated microglia, and loss of synaptic integrity. Amyloid, tau, α-synuclein, TDP-43 and other aggregation-prone proteins converge on shared mechanisms offering common therapeutic leverage points.

04 — Psychiatric Disease

Inflammaging & the Brain

Major depression, bipolar disorder, and schizophrenia are increasingly framed as diseases of accelerated biological aging. Inflammatory cytokines, oxidative stress, HPA axis dysregulation, and microbiome disruption link systemic aging biology to psychiatric phenotypes. Biological psychiatry converges with geroscience.

Neuroplasticity & Performance

Evidence-based interventions for enhancing cognitive function in healthy individuals: sleep architecture optimization, exercise-induced BDNF release, dietary patterns, ketone metabolism, pharmacological and nutraceutical nootropics, and targeted neuromodulation. About This Compilation

Scope

This database covers five interconnected domains: biological aging, oncogenesis, neurodegeneration, psychiatric disease, and cognitive optimization. The organizing thesis is that aging is the primary substrate for all other listed pathologies, and that interventions targeting aging mechanisms have cross-domain therapeutic relevance.

Evidence Standards

Evidence is graded by study type: High (RCTs / large prospective cohorts, n>1000), Moderate (observational human data or replicated mammalian studies), Low (animal model data without human validation), Preliminary (in vitro, computational, or single early-stage animal studies). Effect sizes quantified where available.

Methodology

Primary literature searched via PubMed, bioRxiv, and Google Scholar. No commercial affiliation. No supplement industry funding. Interpretations represent current scientific consensus where it exists; contested claims are flagged explicitly.
