Epigenetic Reprogramming:
Mechanisms, Limits, and Open Questions

Analytical essays on unresolved problems in epigenetic reprogramming and aging biology. Each essay examines a question where the evidence base remains incomplete or contested. Position stated explicitly. Primary literature cited throughout.

01 — Manifesto
On This Project

The pace of progress in longevity research creates pressure to interpret findings as more conclusive than the evidence supports. Mechanisms are described as established before causal structures are resolved. Interventions advance into translational contexts before the underlying biology is fully characterized.

VECVO exists to examine what remains unresolved. Each essay starts from a specific open question in epigenetic reprogramming or aging biology, works through the primary literature, and states a position — with explicit acknowledgment of where evidence is incomplete or contested.

Epigenetic reprogramming is among the most significant developments in aging biology in recent decades. The mechanistic assumptions on which current approaches depend are still being tested. Both the promise and the open questions warrant careful examination.

Stated positions
01Epigenetic clocks correlate with biological age across tissues and species, but the causal relationship between methylation drift and aging remains unresolved. Correlation with a robust biomarker does not establish mechanism.
02Partial reprogramming can reverse epigenetic age markers while the same factors, under different conditions, erase cell identity entirely. The boundary between these outcomes is incompletely characterized.
03The information theory of aging is a productive framework, not a demonstrated mechanism. The distinction has direct implications for which biological level represents the primary therapeutic target.
04Heterochromatin loss with age is reproducible across species. Whether it functions as a driver of aging phenotypes or as a downstream consequence of other damage is not resolved.
02 — Analytical Essays
Open Questions
01 Forthcoming
Are Epigenetic Clocks Measuring the Cause of Aging or Its Shadow?
Thesis: If methylation drift is downstream of upstream molecular damage rather than a primary driver, interventions targeting the clock readout may not address the underlying biology.
Epigenetic clocks show consistent age-correlated methylation patterns across tissues and species. The question of whether this reflects a causal mechanism or a robust downstream signal has not been resolved, and the distinction determines the appropriate therapeutic target.

02 Forthcoming
Partial Reprogramming: Where Does Cell Identity End and Age Begin?
Thesis: The stability of cell identity under transient OSK induction is assumed more than experimentally demonstrated across physiologically relevant contexts.
Yamanaka's original work showed that differentiated cell identity could be erased by defined transcription factors. Partial reprogramming protocols now depend on the assumption that this process can be reliably stopped at an intermediate state. The conditions under which that boundary holds remain incompletely characterized.
~16 min read (est.)
Partial Reprogramming Yamanaka Factors OSK
03 Forthcoming
The Information Theory of Aging: Useful Frame or Established Mechanism?
Thesis: The information theory of aging proposes that epigenetic noise, rather than accumulated molecular lesions, is the primary driver of aging phenotypes. This distinction has not been resolved experimentally, and the choice of framework has direct implications for therapeutic strategy.
If the primary substrate of aging is loss of epigenetic information rather than accumulation of molecular damage, the appropriate intervention target changes substantially. The two frameworks are not equivalent, and the evidence for each deserves independent evaluation.
